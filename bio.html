Profile

Engineering across physical and autonomous systems

I am a mechanical engineer working at the intersection of physical modeling, robotics, sensing, computation, and control. My background spans experimental mechanical engineering, system dynamics, mobile robotics, autonomous systems, and engineering analysis. My academic experience includes mechanical engineering at Pusan National University, graduate research at the University of Victoria, and doctoral-level coursework and robotics research at the University of Waterloo.

My work has ranged from operational modal analysis, vibration and chatter prediction, machining experiments, and engineering data analysis to mobile robot navigation, LiDAR-camera perception, state estimation, path planning, and robotic control. Although these problems come from different engineering domains, they share a common foundation: understanding the physical system, identifying the variables that govern its behavior, and validating conclusions against quantitative evidence.

My current work focuses on robotics and autonomous systems. I develop robotic systems from mathematical and physical models through implementation and validation, combining ROS 2, perception, sensor fusion, localization, SLAM, planning, rigid-body dynamics, control, simulation, and robotic manipulation. Recent projects include EKF-based state estimation, real-time vision perception, LiDAR-camera 3D perception and fusion, and analytical modeling and dynamics cross-validation of a UR5e manipulator.

I approach simulation and numerical results as models to be examined rather than ground truth to be accepted. When analytical calculations, simulation, or implementation disagree, I first identify which assumptions, coordinate conventions, boundary conditions, or interfaces could explain the discrepancy. I prefer independent physical and mathematical cross-validation before modifying a model simply to obtain numerical agreement.

I also integrate AI tools into my engineering workflow for research, implementation, troubleshooting, analysis, and documentation. I use them to accelerate iteration, but not to replace engineering judgment. Assumptions, technical claims, numerical results, data consistency, and source material are independently checked before they are accepted or used in engineering decisions.

My engineering approach is to define the physical problem and its assumptions first, identify the variables that materially affect the system, and then select the appropriate analytical, computational, and experimental tools. I place particular emphasis on reproducibility, quantitative performance measurement, cross-validation, and clear technical documentation so that an engineering result can be explained, tested, and reproduced rather than demonstrated only once.
