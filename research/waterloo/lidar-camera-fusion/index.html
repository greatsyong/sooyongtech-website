LiDAR–Camera Fusion
for Road Segmentation

Camera and LiDAR fusion for semantic road segmentation using a modified SegNet encoder–decoder architecture evaluated on the KITTI road dataset.

Sooyong Kim · Tarun Joseph

Project Overview

Multimodal perception for road detection

The project investigated whether combining RGB camera information with LiDAR-derived depth information could improve semantic road segmentation compared with camera-only perception.

0.9364 Best MaxF · Late Fusion
0.9010 RGB-only MaxF
289 KITTI training samples
96 KITTI test samples
Motivation

Why fuse LiDAR and camera data?

Camera-based semantic segmentation provides dense visual information, but its performance can be affected by illumination, image quality, and environmental conditions. LiDAR provides complementary geometric and distance information that is less dependent on scene appearance.

This project explored the integration of the two sensing modalities for binary road segmentation and compared camera-only processing against two multimodal fusion strategies.

Core tasks

  • KITTI camera and LiDAR data processing
  • LiDAR-to-camera coordinate transformation
  • Depth-map generation
  • Modified SegNet architecture
  • Early fusion
  • Late fusion
  • Quantitative performance comparison
Data Pipeline

LiDAR projection onto the camera plane

KITTI calibration parameters were used to transform Velodyne LiDAR points into the camera coordinate system and project the resulting points onto the image plane.

Processing sequence

The projected LiDAR data were converted into a sparse depth representation aligned with the RGB image and resized for network training and evaluation.

Neural Network

Modified SegNet architecture

The segmentation model was based on the SegNet encoder–decoder architecture and modified for binary road detection.

The network used convolutional blocks with batch normalization, ReLU activation, dropout, and transposed convolution in the decoder for spatial upsampling.

Training configuration

  • Framework: Keras / TensorFlow 2
  • Dataset: KITTI Road
  • Optimizer: Adam
  • Learning rate: 0.0001
  • Batch size: 8
  • Epochs: 30
  • Evaluation: MaxF, Accuracy, IoU
Sensor Fusion

Early fusion vs. late fusion

Two fusion architectures were implemented to evaluate where RGB and LiDAR information should be combined within the segmentation network.

EARLY FUSION

Fusion at the input level

RGB and LiDAR-derived depth information were concatenated before entering the encoder, allowing the network to learn directly from the combined low-level representation.

LATE FUSION

Fusion after independent encoding

RGB and LiDAR data were processed through separate encoder branches and combined at the end of the encoding stage before decoding.

Experimental Results

Fusion improved road segmentation

The late-fusion architecture produced the strongest overall result among the three evaluated sensor configurations.

Configuration MaxF Accuracy IoU Training Time
RGB only 0.9010 0.7828 0.4755 392 s
Early Fusion 0.9153 0.7875 0.4665 392 s
Late Fusion 0.9364 0.7958 0.4898 584 s

Engineering Interpretation

Accuracy versus computational cost

Early fusion improved MaxF relative to the RGB-only configuration without increasing the reported training time.

Late fusion achieved the highest MaxF, accuracy, and IoU, but required two independent encoder streams and therefore increased training time from 392 seconds to 584 seconds.

Key result

MaxF increased from 0.9010 with RGB-only input to 0.9364 with late fusion.

Limitations

Scope of the experiment

The experiments used the KITTI road dataset, which primarily represents favorable road, weather, and lighting conditions.

The project therefore does not establish performance across the full range of adverse conditions that would be encountered by a production autonomous vehicle.

Future work

  • Evaluate more diverse road and weather conditions
  • Investigate real-time segmentation
  • Explore pyramid-based fusion architectures
  • Investigate cross-fusion strategies
