Modeling & Control

UR5e Modeling & Control Program

Four linked studies progressing from model reconciliation to nonlinear plant characterization, controller design, robustness analysis, independent simulation, and ROS 2 runtime validation.

Stage 01 · Model ValidationCompleted

Model Setup & Cross-Validation

Establishes a defensible plant model before controller design by reconciling nominal DH geometry, URDF frames, inertial parameters, fixed-base articulation, and Isaac Sim configuration. Forward kinematics, Jacobians, mass matrices, and gravity generalized forces are independently cross-checked to expose frame, parameter, and simulator inconsistencies rather than absorbing them into later tuning.

Stage 02 · Plant CharacterizationCompleted

Kinematics & Dynamics Characterization

Quantifies the nonlinear structure that governs achievable motion and control authority. The study examines Jacobian conditioning and singularity behavior, pseudoinverse versus damped least-squares inversion, configuration-dependent inertia and gravity loading, inverse dynamics, torque demand, local linearization validity, and trajectory-level numerical consistency across Python and C++ implementations.

Stage 03 · Controller DesignCompleted

Controller Design & Robustness Assessment

Compares PID, computed-torque, LQR, and MPC as different control architectures rather than as a single ranking exercise. Nominal tracking is coupled with model mismatch, disturbance rejection, gravity-feedforward structure, constraint handling, horizon and tolerance sensitivity, computational cost, and Python–C++ cross-validation to identify where each method's assumptions become operationally significant.

Stage 04 · Runtime ValidationCompleted

Independent Simulation & Runtime Validation

Tests whether analytically validated controllers retain their behavior after crossing the simulation and software boundary. Independent Isaac Sim validation and ROS 2/C++ execution are used to examine model-fidelity gaps, control-loop timing, computation latency, actuator interaction, MPC deadline and completion failures, and the distinction between a mathematically valid design and a practically executable controller.
